Use a reference workflow when you want to discuss a song's groove, atmosphere or structure and build your own parts. Use a stem remix when you intend to rearrange audio retained from the source. BeatMind supports both kinds of planning, but they are not interchangeable.

Choosing the workflow first makes the next decision clearer: are you borrowing a production idea, or using the recording itself?

## For an original track, describe the qualities you want

A useful brief names what attracts you and what should be different. For example:

> I like the spacious percussion and gradual energy changes. Build an original deep minimal track at 124 BPM with my own sounds. Avoid reusing the source vocal or melody. Start with one clean kick and let me review it.

That is more actionable than asking for an exact copy. It distinguishes the reference's role from the samples, instruments and notes you want in your own set.

Reference analysis can inform the discussion. It does not reveal the artist's original project, every device setting or the intention behind every sound.

## Review stems and timing before building a plan

In **References**, upload audio you are permitted to process and wait for analysis to finish. Listen to the original mix alongside the available separated layers. Stems are estimates: percussion, bass or vocal content can bleed into another layer.

The displayed separation model determines which layers are available. Do not assume that a drums stem also contains clean, independent kick, snare and hi-hat files. Review the actual files and save the choices you want to use.

In **Timing**, check the estimated BPM and section boundaries against playback before confirming them. Half-time interpretations, pickup notes and gradual transitions can make an automated estimate misleading. Confirmation should reflect your listening, not simply acceptance of a number.

## Give AI listening a focused brief

In **Listening**, describe a question such as "How does the bass leave room for the kick?" or "Where does the percussion become denser?" Select the offered listening scope and grant the separate audio-sharing permission before submitting the request.

Audio listening is an interpretation. Treat statements about texture and possible effects as listening notes, not proof of an exact synthesizer, key, compressor setting or studio signal chain. Compare the notes with the section you hear and correct the brief when necessary.

## A template is not finished music

Use **Template** to decide the roles, section timing and musical direction. A saved or approved plan is not evidence that Ableton contains the complete result.

Build and review parts in chat, checking that the current Live Set matches the intended project. Later, use **Compare** where available to compare a captured audition with a reference layer. A level-matched comparison can make judgment easier; it is still your decision whether the sound belongs in the track.

## For a stem remix, render the source layers deliberately

Choose **Automatic Remix** when you want a preview made from existing reference stems. You control section order, repeats, layer levels and target tempo, then listen and approve before downloading aligned audio.

This release does not automatically place that approved remix into Ableton. It also does not generate new replacement instruments. Follow the [Automatic Remix guide](/blog/automatic-remix-preview-guide) for the exact released steps and limitations.

Retaining source audio also means retaining responsibility for its use. Confirm the permissions needed for your intended use before uploading or publishing; a BeatMind checkbox is not a licence from a rights holder.

## FAQ

### Are separated stems the artist's original studio tracks?

No. They are estimates extracted from a mixed recording and may contain bleed or artifacts. They do not expose the original project or effect settings.

### Does AI listening identify every instrument and effect exactly?

No. Listening notes are interpretations and may be uncertain or wrong. Use them to ask better questions and compare audible results, not as an exact reconstruction of a studio session.

### Is saving a template the same as creating the song in Ableton?

No. A template or brief records the plan. Check the actual Ableton tracks, clips and audio before treating production as complete.

### Which workflow should I choose for my own sounds?

Use a reference-inspired original brief and build new parts in chat. Choose a stem remix only when you intend to retain source audio and have the permissions needed for that use.
